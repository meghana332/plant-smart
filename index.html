How Plant Smart works

  1. ScanYou take a photo of the plant tag or a leaf at the nursery.
  2. IdentifyThe AI reads the words on the tag and looks at the leaf shape to work out the tree type, and whether it is a male, female or low-pollen variety.
  3. RateIt gives the tree a pollen score from 1 (almost no allergy pollen) to 10 (a pollen bomb).
  4. SuggestIf the tree is a bad choice, it suggests low-pollen trees that grow well where you live.
Tag secret: "seedless" and "fruitless"

Words like seedless, fruitless, cottonless or podless on a tag often mean the tree is a male clone. Male trees make no messy fruit, but they make lots of pollen. That is the 1950s city-planning problem from our research.

Good to know for judges' questions

Only some tree species have separate male and female trees (like ash, mulberry, box elder, willow and ginkgo). Others, like oak and birch, have both kinds of flowers on one tree. The scores here are approximate and follow the idea of the Ogren Plant Allergy Scale (OPALS), which rates plants from 1 to 10. AI can make mistakes, so a real arborist should confirm before anyone plants or grafts.

AI settings (API key)

To scan photos, Plant Smart needs a Claude API key. Ask a parent or coach to create one at console.anthropic.com. It works on Wi-Fi or mobile data. The key is saved only in this app on this device. On iPhone and iPad, enter it inside the installed app, because it doesn't share keys with Safari.
